RESEARCH INFORMATION SHEET

Effects of Response–Reward Contingencies on Behavioral Variability in Humans

INTRODUCTION

Megan Cady, Principal Investigator, and Dr. Aaron P. Blaisdell, Faculty Sponsor, from the UCLA Department of Psychology are conducting a research study. It is not being externally funded. You were selected as a possible participant because you are eligible to participate through the UCLA Psychology Department Subject Pool (SONA), are at least 18 years old, and have access to a computer or laptop with an internet connection. Your participation in this research study is voluntary.

WHAT SHOULD I KNOW ABOUT A RESEARCH STUDY?

  • The information below explains this research study.
  • Whether or not you take part is up to you.
  • You can choose not to take part.
  • You can agree to take part and later change your mind.
  • Your decision will not be held against you.
  • You can ask questions before you decide.

WHY IS THIS RESEARCH BEING DONE?

The purpose of this research is to learn more about how people behave during a computer-based task and how patterns of responding may change as people experience different relationships between their actions and outcomes.

HOW LONG WILL THE RESEARCH LAST AND WHAT WILL I NEED TO DO?

Participation will take a total of approximately 30 minutes and will occur entirely online in one session.

If you volunteer to participate in this study, you will be asked to:

  • Read the study instructions and complete an online computer-based behavioral game. The game will take approximately 20 minutes.
  • Interact with the game and attempt to earn rewards by responding on the screen.
  • Complete a brief post-task survey about your experience with the task.
  • Complete the study using your own computer or laptop in a quiet, private location of your choosing.

The behavioral task is experimental. Some details about how the task works cannot be fully explained before participation because knowing those details in advance could affect how you respond. You will receive additional information about the purpose and design of the task after you finish the study.

ARE THERE ANY RISKS IF I PARTICIPATE?

This study involves no more than minimal risk. You may experience mild boredom, fatigue, or frustration while completing the computer-based task. You may stop participating at any time.

ARE THERE ANY BENEFITS IF I PARTICIPATE?

You will not directly benefit from your participation in the research.

The results of the research may contribute to a better scientific understanding of how patterns of behavior change under different relationships between actions and outcomes.

WHAT OTHER CHOICES DO I HAVE IF I CHOOSE NOT TO PARTICIPATE?

You do not have to participate in this study. You may choose not to participate or may choose to participate in a different study or use any other option for earning credit offered through the UCLA Psychology Department Subject Pool (SONA).

HOW WILL INFORMATION ABOUT ME AND MY PARTICIPATION BE KEPT CONFIDENTIAL?

The researchers will do their best to make sure that your private information is kept confidential. Information about you will be handled as confidentially as possible, but participating in research may involve a loss of privacy and the potential for a breach in confidentiality. Study data will be electronically secured. As with any use of electronic means to store data, there is a risk of breach of data security.

Use of personal information that can identify you:

The behavioral research dataset will not contain your name, email address, or other direct identifiers. You will be represented in the research dataset by a study-specific participant code. SONA is used only to manage sign-ups and research participation credit, and your name and SONA identification number will not be recorded with your task responses.

How information about you will be stored:

Behavioral data from the online task will be transmitted through DataPipe and stored in an access-restricted folder in the research team’s UCLA Google Drive. Data transmitted over the internet will be encrypted during transmission using HTTPS. Access to the stored research data will be limited to authorized study personnel.

People and agencies that will have access to your information:

The research team and authorized UCLA personnel may have access to study data and records to monitor the study. Publications and/or presentations that result from this study will not identify you by name.

How long information from the study will be kept:

De-identified research data may be retained after completion of the study for future research and analysis. The retained dataset will not contain your name, email address, or other direct identifiers.

USE OF DATA FOR FUTURE RESEARCH

Your de-identified data may be kept for use in future research. De-identified data may also be shared with other researchers or posted in a public research data repository (such as OSF), for example when the results are published. These data will not include your name or other information that directly identifies you. Any future use or sharing of study data will be limited to de-identified data and will occur in accordance with applicable UCLA requirements and institutional policies.

WILL I BE PAID FOR MY PARTICIPATION?

You will not receive monetary payment for participating. You will receive 0.5 research participation credits through the UCLA Psychology Department Subject Pool (SONA) for participating in this approximately 30-minute study. You will receive this credit even if you choose to stop before finishing.

DEBRIEFING STATEMENT

Effects of Response–Reward Contingencies on Behavioral Variability in Humans

Thank you for participating in this study.

The purpose of this research is to examine how different relationships between behavior and rewards influence behavioral variability. Some information about the specific response–reward relationships in the task was not provided to you before participation because knowing these details in advance could have influenced how you responded during the task.

During the task, the availability of points and their relationship to your responses varied across conditions. Depending on your condition, points may have been delivered with different probabilities, may have been signaled by cues on the screen, and may have depended on your responses, been prevented by your responses, or been delivered regardless of what you did. Participants were randomly assigned to conditions, and some participants experienced more than one condition. Comparing these conditions allows us to examine whether people change where, when, and how they respond depending on how often rewards are available and how their behavior relates to receiving them.

We did not provide false information about the study before you participated. Rather, we withheld some details about the specific response–reward relationships so that we could measure behavior without participants intentionally changing their responses based on knowledge of the experimental design.

Please do not discuss the specific purpose or conditions of this study with other students who may participate in the future, as knowing this information in advance could influence their behavior and affect the results of the study.

If you have questions about your rights as a research participant or concerns about the research, you may contact the UCLA Office of the Human Research Protection Program (OHRPP) at (310) 206-2040 or participants@research.ucla.edu.
